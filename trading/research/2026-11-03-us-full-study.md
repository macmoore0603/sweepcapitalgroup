# US full study, 2026-11-03

An independent check re-read 20 key prop-firm figures on their sources and confirmed 19; it also recomputed the COT study from a fresh CFTC download and reproduced it, with one correction. A second check compared every figure in this note with the gathered sources and re-read five more live (the My Forex Funds quote, UProfit's figures and three IRS rules); all five matched.

This extends [US evidence](2026-11-03-us-evidence.md), which already covers the CFTC retail-futures study, day-trader base rates and the basic My Forex Funds facts.

## What the firms' own documents say about the funded account

- **Topstep:** the Terms call the funds for simulated trading "fictitious". The Combine and Express Funded Account are described as simulations.
- **Tradeify:** account funds are "exclusively demo funds unless otherwise stated" and stay the company's property. Payments run through a payment agent, Novaflame LTD.
- **MyFundedFutures:** "All MFFU accounts operate in a simulated environment", and "Payouts are based on simulated account profits".
- **Take Profit Trader:** "all PRO accounts are simulated", yet withdrawn profits "are absolutely real". PRO+ is a live-market account.
- **Earn2Trade:** the homepage says "We fund traders to trade the futures markets with our capital". But 94.77% of 2025 passers got LiveSim, which its help center calls "a simulated trading account" that allows withdrawals.
- **Apex:** "All trading conducted in Evaluation and Performance Accounts is simulated", and rewards are at its discretion. Its meta description says "trade our capital… Stop risking your own money."
- **Bulenox:** Master Account trading "is simulated only"; the homepage says "Trade our capital, not your risk."
- **Elite Trader Funding:** every account except LIVE ELITE holds "synthetic currency", and rewards are "incentives".
- **Lucid:** trading is simulated until a trader is moved to "a live brokerage account funded with our capital".
- **Leeloo and DayTraders.com:** accounts "may represent simulated accounts or copied accounts" (Leeloo) or "simulated, live, or mirrored trading accounts" (DayTraders.com).

## Where payouts come from

- **No firm's documents say** whether payouts are funded by fees or by trading profits. Topstep's Terms name no source in the part re-read. Bulenox "may make a payment from any account at any time in any amount."
- **Alpha Futures, July 2026:** after closing its Premium plan, its first batch of owed Premium payouts was 10% of the amount outstanding (firm post, 15 Jul 2026).
- **The only regulator statement** is the CFTC's 2023 complaint against My Forex Funds, a forex firm: "substantially all of the payments come from fees paid by other customers, in a manner similar to a Ponzi scheme" ([CFTC complaint](https://www.cftc.gov/media/9196/enftradersglobalgroupcomplaint082923/download)). This was never adjudicated.
- **Private suits (unverified; docket pages could not be fetched):** McCrudden v. TopstepFunded (M.D. Fla. 3:26-cv-00816, filed Apr 2026, discovery stayed Aug 2026) and Lawton v. Apex (W.D. Tex. 1:26-cv-02078, filed Jul 2026) allege unregistered commodity pools. These are allegations only.

## US enforcement on trading education, signals and prop firms

- **RagingBull.com** (FTC, sued Dec 2020): it advertised "DOUBLE or TRIPLE your account in One Week!"
  - The FTC alleged at least $137M in losses over three years, later more than $197M, and said the company did not track whether subscribers made any money.
  - It settled in March 2022 for $2.425M, and refunds of more than $2.4M went out in March 2023. New Hampshire fined it $675,000 (Dec 2021).
  - Guru Kyle Dennis, who allegedly took more than $13.6M, got only an injunction (Sept 2023): after *AMG Capital v. FTC* the FTC could not seek his money.
- **Online Trading Academy** (FTC, Feb 2020): courses cost up to $50,000 and brought in more than $370M over six years. Trading data suggested "the vast majority" of customers made no money.
  - The Sept 2020 settlement set a $362M judgment, partly suspended for inability to pay; the founder paid $8.3M.
  - Refunds (Aug 2021) were more than $5.4M to 31,144 people, about $175 each, plus more than $13.3M of debt forgiven.
- **IM Mastery Academy** (FTC and Nevada AG, May 2025): more than $1.2B taken in. The FTC said IML's own data showed 60% of customers quit paying within a month and 90% within six months, and "many, if not most" MLM participants lost money. In May 2026 the leaders accepted a $795.8M judgment, surrendered assets worth nearly $90M and were banned from selling trading training.
- **DK Automation** (FTC, Nov 2022): sold a "#1 secret passive income crypto trading bot". At least $2.6M went to refunds under a judgment of nearly $53M.
- **Finfluencers (SEC and DOJ):**
  - Eight Twitter/Discord traders were charged in Dec 2022 over an alleged pump-and-dump that made them about $100M in profits (SEC; the indictment says $114M). A district court dismissed the indictment, the Fifth Circuit reversed it (Oct 2025), and trial is set for May 2027. One defendant pleaded guilty; the rest pleaded not guilty, and nothing is proven.
  - Kim Kardashian paid $1.26M (SEC, Oct 2022) for not disclosing a $250,000 paid post.
  - An SEC advisory committee (Dec 2024) cited a CFA study: only 20% of finfluencer recommendation content carried any disclosure.
- **"AI signal" groups (SEC charges, not proven):** the SEC alleged that WhatsApp investment clubs misappropriated at least $14M through fake platforms (Dec 2025), and that BG Wealth took more than $130M from tens of thousands of investors, backed by false Form ADV and Form D filings (Oct 2026).
- **Signal and copy-trade sellers (CFTC):**
  - IML's fxsignalslive.com mirrored trades into about 500 customers' accounts without registration, for a $150,000 penalty; Wealth Generators also paid $150,000 (both Sept 2018).
  - FuturesFX's "live trading room" showed only simulated trades: $1.3M restitution plus a $450,000 penalty (Apr 2020).
  - Lions of Forex, fronted by a "seven-figure trader", was ordered to pay about $172,000 restitution plus more than $516,000 in penalties (announced Jan 2025).
- **Bots (CFTC):** Mirror Trading International took 29,421 BTC (over $1.73B) from more than 23,000 US participants through a fictional "bot" and misappropriated all of it; its founder got a default judgment of more than $1.7B restitution plus more than $1.7B in penalties (Apr 2023). ROFX had no robot at all: more than $56M restitution plus a $169M penalty (Apr 2024).
- **CFTC advisory (Jan 2024):** be wary of bot and signal claims of "tens of thousands of percent" or 100% "win" rates.
- **My Forex Funds, beyond the US evidence note:** the CFTC also alleged the firm was the counterparty to substantially all trades despite saying "we only make money when you do". It alleged that more than 70% of customers moved to real-market routing then made less. The court awarded the defendants $3,148,484.22 in fees and costs (July 2025), and the CFTC's acting chairman called the agency's own conduct "willful and bad faith" (May 2025).
- **States, FINRA, CFPB:** Texas halted YieldTrust.ai (Apr 2023), an AI bot promising up to 2.2% a day whose developers could block withdrawals, and BG Wealth (June 2026), whose "zero-risk" signals carried a 12% "exit tax". NASAA named AI trading bots a top threat (Mar 2025). FINRA fined M1 Finance $850,000 over about 1,700 paid influencers (Mar 2024). No CFPB alert was found that targets courses, signals, bots or prop firms.
- **The pattern:** sellers promised fast or guaranteed returns, kept no record of typical customers' results (RagingBull, OTA, IML) or passed off simulated trades as live (FuturesFX). Large judgments were often suspended (OTA) or entered by default (MTI, ROFX).

## Do NQ small traders predict next-week returns? (CFTC COT)

- **Data:** CFTC Commitments of Traders, Legacy futures-only, from the [CFTC Public Reporting Environment](https://publicreporting.cftc.gov/) (dataset 6dca-aqww); the cftc.gov history files returned HTTP 403. E-mini Nasdaq-100 code 209742: 1,428 weekly reports, June 1999 to Oct 2026. The Micro contract (from Aug 2020) is used only in a robustness check. Outcomes use NQ daily closes, with the FRED Nasdaq-100 cash index as a roll-free check.
- **Method, pre-registered before download** (the calendar rules below were added later; see caveats):
  - The signal is S = (non-reportable long − non-reportable short) / open interest from the Tuesday report, plus its weekly change.
  - Entry is the last close of the release week (release Friday 15:30 ET, moved for holidays) and exit is a week later. Reports from three government-shutdown backlogs (29) were dropped, as were 108 holiday-delayed reports that shared an entry close with a newer one and 65 dated before the price data. The sample is 1,225 weeks, Sept 2000 to Oct 2026: 681 before 2015 and 544 from 2015.
  - Tests: correlation and OLS slope with Newey-West t, against a bar of |t| ≥ 1.96.
  - A contrarian rule goes short when S is in the top fifth of the prior 156 reports and long in the bottom fifth, at 0.375 index points per unit change in position (1,142 weeks from June 2002).
  - A P&L proxy multiplies net non-reportable contracts by the Tuesday-to-Tuesday price change and $20 (1,362 weeks).
- **Level:** full sample corr −0.064, NW t −1.86 (p 0.062), R² 0.41%. Each 10-point rise in S (as a share of open interest) went with −0.20% the next week. Before 2015 t −1.75; from 2015 t −0.61.
- **Change:** NW t −1.27 in the full sample and −0.50 from 2015.
- **The effect is all 2000-2002:** those 109 weeks give level t −2.34. For 2003-2026 (1,116 weeks) corr is +0.0005, t +0.02.
- **Contrarian rule:** −1.70% a year gross (t −0.64) and −1.82% net, against +13.15% a year for buy-and-hold (Sharpe 0.65); hit rate 46.6%. Weeks after a top-fifth S averaged +0.576%, against +0.352% after a bottom-fifth S. That is the opposite of the contrarian idea, and not significant (t 0.85).
- **P&L proxy:** small traders lost about $1.2B on E-mini, Sept 2000 to Oct 2026, but the weekly mean cannot be told from zero (NW t −0.56). Their average net long (3,863 contracts, 1.5% of open interest) earned +$2,009M and their timing lost $3,211M (t −1.43). Since 2015 the sign depends on method: −$142M E-mini only, +$147M with Micro.
- **Verdict:** by the pre-registered bar there is no reliable evidence that small traders' NQ positioning predicts next-week returns. They were modestly on the wrong side in 2000-2002. Since 2003 there is no exploitable signal and no consistent wrong-side or right-side record.

Caveats:

- **Proxy:** non-reportable positions include small funds and prop traders, not only retail, and leave out options, ETFs and (outside one check) the Micro contract.
- **Calendar choices matter:** the holiday, shutdown and same-entry rules depart from the pre-registration to remove lookahead. The literal pre-registered version gives t −2.02 (p 0.044). Release timing for 2000-2002, the period that drives the effect, could not be checked.
- **Many tests:** the one near-significant result (p 0.06) would not survive a multiple-testing correction.
- **Prices and P&L:** the NQ series is not back-adjusted, but the roll-free index and roll-week exclusions give the same conclusions. The P&L proxy uses Tuesday snapshots and ignores intra-week trading and fees.

What the independent recomputation found:

- **Reproduced:** separate code on a fresh CFTC download matched every headline statistic to the reported precision, and the data matched with 0 differences. Small gaps: same-week change t +3.04 against +2.88, AR(1) 0.864 against 0.85, and pre-2015 timing −$1,083M against −$1,100M.
- **One correction:** the study said the bar is crossed only in versions that let in lookahead. A stricter calendar with no lookahead (any holiday in the report week delays the release to Monday; 1,075 weeks) gives level t −2.19. Significance is fragile in both directions; from 2003 that calendar gives t −0.24.
- **Its own calendar** (1,227 weeks) gives level t −1.77. With entry one week later, the 2000-2002 effect disappears (t +0.29).
- **Unresolved:** four weeks (around the 2005, 2017 and 2021 Inaugurations and the Feb 2010 snow closures) may contain lookahead if CFTC releases were delayed. This could not be verified.
- **The verdict stands:** in every variant the 2003-2026 effect is about zero and the contrarian rule loses money.

## US tax: prop payouts vs futures trading

General information, not tax advice. Sources are IRS pages and the firms' help pages, read Nov 2026.

- **Futures in your own account:** regulated futures are Section 1256 contracts ([IRS Form 6781](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f6781.pdf), 2025). Open contracts are marked to market on the last business day of the year. Gains and losses are 60% long-term and 40% short-term whatever the holding period, and wash-sale rules do not apply. They are reported on Form 6781 Part I from box 11 of the broker's 1099-B (IRS Pub 550, 2025).
- **Rates (2025, single filer):** long-term 0%, 15% and 20% (20% above $533,400); the top ordinary rate is 37%, from $626,351 (IRS). Arithmetic estimate, not an IRS figure: the blended top rate on 1256 gains is about 26.8%, before the 3.8% NIIT. NIIT covers trading income above $200,000 MAGI, or $250,000 joint (IRS Topic 559).
- **Losses:** net capital losses offset other income by up to $3,000 a year, $1,500 married filing separately (IRS Topic 409). Individuals can elect to carry a net 1256 loss back 3 years (Form 6781).
- **No self-employment tax** applies to own-account trading gains, with or without a 475(f) election; options and commodities dealers are the exception (Pub 550).
- **Prop payouts are reported as contractor income.** Topstep and Apex use Form 1099-NEC and call traders independent contractors. Tradeify 1099s, and MyFundedFutures 1099s for payouts made through Rise, come from the processor Rise, and Elite Trader Funding uses 1099-MISC. Take Profit Trader says payouts are "often" reported as contractor-style income "rather than as capital gains". Topstep says its 1099-NEC shows the gross payout before fees, and that the fee goes on Schedule C.
- **What that means:** the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-defined) says independent contractors are self-employed.
  - Payouts carry ordinary income tax plus 15.3% self-employment tax (12.4% Social Security, 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net earnings, owed once net earnings reach $400.
  - The Social Security part stops at $184,500 (2026), a 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies above $200,000 single or $250,000 joint, and half the tax is deductible.
  - Income goes on Schedule C and Schedule SE. Firms do not withhold, and estimated payments are generally due if you expect to owe $1,000 or more.
- **1099 threshold:** for tax years after 2025 the 1099-NEC threshold rose from $600 to $2,000 (IRS instructions, 12/2026). All business income must still be reported, with or without a form (Pub 334, 2025).
- **Evaluation fees** (inference from general rules; no IRS page found names them):
  - If the activity is a business, fees are deductible on Schedule C when "ordinary and necessary". Fees paid before the business starts may be startup costs: up to $5,000 in the first year, phased out above $50,000 of total startup costs, the rest over 180 months.
  - If it is a hobby, the income is still reported. Pub 529 (12/2020) says the expenses "can no longer be deducted", while Pub 525 (2025) describes the limit differently.
- **Trader tax status and 475(f):** status depends on facts: seeking profit from daily price moves, with substantial, continuous and regular activity. Calling yourself a day trader does not decide it (IRS Topic 429).
  - A 475(f) election makes gains ordinary (Form 4797), removes the loss limits and wash-sale rules, and must be made by the original due date of the prior year's return. Late elections are generally not allowed.
  - A reading of the statute (§475(d)(1), (f)(2)) that no IRS publication read states outright: a commodities election would replace 60/40 with ordinary treatment.
  - Both describe trading for your own account. For simulated prop accounts paid as contractor income they probably do not fit (inference).
- **No IRS guidance specific to prop firms was found** (unverified; a search result). Payout treatment rests on the firms' reporting and the general contractor rules.

## For an NQ prop-firm day trader

- **Most starters were not paid.** Firm-published, unaudited figures imply about 8-17% of starters were paid at least once (arithmetic estimate for MyFundedFutures, Tradeify and Topstep). Only 0.71-4.2% of funded traders at those three firms reached a live account.
- **The funded account is usually simulated,** by the firms' own documents. No firm says where payouts come from. The only regulator filing on the question alleged that one forex firm's payouts came from other customers' fees, and it was never decided.
- **Small-trader positioning is not an NQ signal.** Twenty-six years of COT data show no reliable next-week prediction since 2003, and a contrarian rule lost money against buy-and-hold.
- **A payout is reported as contractor income** by the firms, so under the general IRS contractor rules it carries ordinary rates plus 15.3% self-employment tax, not the 60/40 rates that futures in your own account get. No IRS guidance specific to prop firms was found.
- **Course, signal and bot sellers that show no typical-customer results** fit the pattern regulators keep finding (RagingBull, OTA, IML, FuturesFX).
