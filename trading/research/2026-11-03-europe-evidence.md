# Europe evidence, 2026-11-03

An independent check re-read 20 key figures on their sources and confirmed all 20; for one (Entrop et al. 2016) only the headline finding was confirmed. A second check compared every figure in this note with the gathered sources and re-read five more live (ESMA 2018 and 2019, AMF 2014, FCA PS19/18, FPFX); all five matched.

## Academic studies of active retail traders

- **Finland, day traders** (Linnainmaa, "The Individual Day Trader", working paper 2005; 22,529 day traders among 1,055,505 investors, January 1995 to November 2002):
  - Only 3 in 10 made money after costs in a typical month. The typical day trader lost about EUR 100 a day after fees.
  - The average day trader lost even before fees. The more active ones were profitable before fees and lost after them.
  - After losing day trades, traders bet smaller or quit (Linnainmaa, "Learning from Experience", 2005).
- **Finland, limit orders** (Linnainmaa, *JF* 2010 and working paper 2005; all Finnish individual investors, September 1998 to October 2001): households' limit orders lost EUR 74.7m (EUR 169 per investor), against EUR 17.7m (EUR 40) on market orders. Their limit orders filled in the first 2 minutes after earnings news lost 2.5% on average the same day.
- **Finland, registry studies:** households underperformed and foreign investors outperformed, December 1994 to December 1996 (Grinblatt & Keloharju, *JFE* 2000). High-IQ investors beat low-IQ investors by 2.2% a year, partly because low-IQ investors' limit orders were picked off more (Grinblatt, Keloharju & Linnainmaa, *JFE* 2012; eight years of trades).
- **Netherlands, online broker** (Bauer, Cosemans & Eichholtz, *JBF* 2009; 68,146 accounts, January 2000 to March 2006):
  - Option traders lost 1.81% a month gross and 4.46% net, against 0.58% and 1.57% for stock-only investors. An option trade cost about EUR 32 on average, a stock trade EUR 22.
  - Results persisted, mostly on the losing side: the top deciles' alphas were "positive but insignificant", while the bottom two deciles' alphas were significantly negative.
- **Netherlands, technical analysis** (Hoffmann & Shefrin, *JEBO* 2014; Dutch discount-broker clients, 2000-2006): clients using it earned about 50 basis points a month less from poor selection, plus 20 bp a month of turnover-linked cost. For heavy options traders ("high derivative rollers") it was 140 bp plus 29 bp.
- **Germany, leveraged certificates:**
  - DAX warrants and knock-outs (Baller, Entrop, Schober & Wilkens, Passau discussion paper; 3,000+ investors, about half a million trades, April 2007 to December 2008): round trips lost on average about 4% net (2% gross) on warrants and 6% net (4% gross) on knock-outs. Median knock-out holding was 0.05 trading days. Timing skill was neutral, and issuer pricing and spreads drove the losses.
  - Boerse Stuttgart (Meyer, Schroff & Weinhardt, 2013 working paper of the *FMPM* 2014 article; April 2009 to February 2010): single-stock leverage trades returned -4.96% to -9.45% raw, by holding period. Index trades made money over some holding periods while the DAX rose 30%, with average Sharpe ratios below 0.3.
  - Structured products (Entrop et al., *RQFA* 2016; 10,652 investors): negative alphas even before costs, worse for more complex products.
- **Germany, self-assessment:** among 215 online-broker investors, the correlation between estimated and actual returns over 1997-2000 was about zero (-0.05). Fewer than 5% thought they had lost money; more than 25% had (Glaser & Weber, *FRL* 2007). Those who rated themselves above average traded more without better past results (Glaser & Weber, *Geneva Risk and Insurance Review* 2007), and those who thought they knew more than average churned more (Dorn & Huberman, *Review of Finance* 2005; 1,345 surveyed clients of a large online broker, 1995-2000).
- **UK, index spread bets** (Ma et al., *EJOR* 2022; 5,164 clients of one firm betting on FTSE 100 and DAX futures, March 2006 to February 2012): only 25% had a positive average return per trade. The least and the most profitable were both the most likely to quit. A Southampton PhD thesis (Gulthawatvichai 2015) finds FTSE 100 spread traders add to losing positions after losses.
- **Gap:** no peer-reviewed European study was found that uses account-level data on retail micro-futures day traders or on funded-trader participants.

## Regulators' outcome data on leveraged products

- **CFDs across the EU** ([ESMA, March 2018](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors)): 74-89% of retail accounts lost, with average losses of EUR 1,600 to 29,000 per client.
  - National studies, as summarised by BaFin (2019): CySEC 76% of about 290,000 accounts (January-August 2017); CNMV about 82% (2015-16); Central Bank of Ireland 74-75% (2013-16); CONSOB 75-83% (2014-16); KNF Poland 79-82% every year 2012-2017.
  - France ([AMF 2014](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_etude_analyse/epargne_prestataire/Etude%20des%20resultats%20des%20investisseurs%20particuliers%20sur%20le%20trading%20de%20CFD%20et%20de%20Forex%20en%20France.pdf); 14,799 clients, 2009-2012, one firm 2010-2013): 89.4% lost; mean -EUR 10,887, median -EUR 1,843. Explicit fees were only 14.2% of losses (fee data from some firms only; spreads not counted). Clients with 250+ orders lost EUR 18,741 on average. A fixed cohort of 1,881 went from 82.5% losing in 2009 to 87.6% over 2009-2012.
  - UK (FCA CP16/40, 2016; sample from eight firms, spread bets included): 82% lost over a year, on average GBP 2,200. Inexperienced clients lost less because they traded less.
- **The 2018 leverage caps** (30:1 down to 2:1, 50% margin close-out) left the share of losers broadly unchanged:
  - ESMA (2019, 2020): the share of profitable accounts "remained broadly stable" from November-January 2017-18 to 2018-19, while costs fell.
  - FCA (PS19/18, 2019): UK client losses fell GBP 77m in August-October 2018, but the share of loss-making accounts "may not improve". At one firm that cut leverage, percentage P&L per trade, net of costs, showed no significant change and trade value fell about 28% (CP18/38, 2018).
  - CNMV (July 2023): 70-90% of Spanish CFD clients still lose, depending on the firm.
  - ESMA's CFD definition excludes futures (statement, February 2026). Spain is the one exception found: a CNMV resolution of July 2023 limits leverage on retail futures and options and requires a close-out below 50% of initial margin.
- **Netherlands, turbos** ([AFM 2020](https://afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/onderwerpen/turbo/afm-turbos-report-eng.pdf?la=nl-NL); four large distributors, June 2017 to June 2018):
  - 68% of clients lost, on average EUR 2,680. 88% of clients with more than 500 transactions lost, against 64% of those with fewer than 10.
  - 54% of trades won, yet the average trade lost 2.9%: the average loser was -32.8% and the average winner +22.6%.
- **Germany, turbos** ([BaFin 2025](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Veroeffentlichungen/DE/Fachartikel/2025/Studie_250521_Turbo_Zertifikate.html); about 543,000 investors, about 113m transactions, 2019-2023):
  - 74.2% lost, on average EUR 6,358, over EUR 3.4bn in total. The average round trip returned -2%.
  - The loss rate rose with activity: about 70% for 1-10 transactions, 83% for 100-500, 91% for over 1,000. BaFin saw "apparently no learning process".
  - About 70% of positions were held under 24 hours. BaFin's turbo restrictions apply from 16 June 2026.
- **Germany, futures** (BaFin survey, July 2019 to June 2020, cited in its 2022 futures product intervention): on average more than half of retail clients lost, over 75% at some providers, measured per position. About 70,000 clients traded Eurex futures in 2021; about a quarter of their transactions were in mini or micro contracts.
- **Brokers' required disclosures** (pages read 3 November 2026; past 12 months, net of all costs, recalculated quarterly): 55% (eToro) to 82% (Plus500) of retail CFD accounts lost money.

## Prop-firm and "funded trader" warnings

- **Spain, CNMV (June 2019):** "funded trading accounts" sold with courses carry a risk of fraud and fall outside its remit.
- **Czech Republic, ČNB (September 2023; FTMO's home):** purely simulated demo trading is not an investment service and needs no licence. Rewards that depend on demo results would have to be assessed under derivatives law.
- **Belgium, [FSMA (March 2024)](https://www.fsma.be/en/warnings/fsma-warns-consumers-against-shadow-investment-game):** a "shadow investment game". Challenges are costly and often repeated, "the consumer never makes any actual trades", and the firms hold no authorisation.
- **Italy, CONSOB (July 2024):** a "finance video game". Complaints say tests are contrived to push repeat attempts and profits go unpaid.
- **UK, FCA (August 2025):** put "Funded Trader" (funded-trader.net) on its Warning List, meaning no Ombudsman or FSCS protection. No general FCA prop-firm policy was found.
- **Netherlands, [AFM (February 2026)](https://www.afm.nl/en/sector/actueel/2026/jan/pb-ufunded):** the first enforcement finding against a prop model that was found. Ufunded's "funding" of 30 times the fee was unlicensed CFD order execution; 1,279 Dutch clients paid more than EUR 20m; "Ufunded earns money whenever customers lose".
- **ESMA:** no prop-firm statement was found. CySEC's chair said in July 2026 that, to the best of his knowledge, ESMA is "not currently engaged in any substantive discussions regarding retail prop trading". A reported ESMA "joint statement" on prop firms of 10 September 2026 is not confirmed (unverified): it appears only in prop-industry blogs and was not found on ESMA's site.
- **Pass and payout rates** (FPFX, a prop-firm technology vendor, via Finance Magnates, September 2024; 10 firms, 100,000 traders, 300,000+ accounts): 14% passed, about 45% of those were paid (7% of all), and the average payout was 4% of plan size. A June 2026 CySEC document re-quotes the 7%. This is industry data, not audited and not Europe-specific.

## For an NQ prop-firm day trader

- **Most lose:** 68-89% of clients in the CFD and turbo studies, rising to 88-91% at 500+ transactions (AFM, BaFin). AMF and BaFin found no learning effect, and in the FCA sample experienced clients lost more.
- **Costs scale with trade count but are not the whole loss.** More active Finnish day traders were profitable before fees and lost after them; in France explicit fees were 14.2% of losses, with spreads not counted.
- **A high win rate is not an edge:** 54% of Dutch turbo trades won, yet the average trade lost, because losers were larger than winners (AFM).
- **A challenge is a fee-paid simulation without investor protection.** Regulators say the model earns from repeat attempts, and the only payout figure found, 7% of buyers, is unaudited vendor data.
